# Purchase orders and landed cost

A **purchase order** records what you ordered from a supplier. When the stock arrives, you
receive it, and two things happen: the units go on your shelf, and each product's **cost** is
updated to what it really cost you to get it there: the price you paid **plus** its share of
freight, customs duty and clearing. That true cost is the **landed cost**.

You find all of this under **Stock**: **Purchase orders**, **Inbound shipments** and
**Suppliers**.

## What this is for

- Knowing your real margin. A mouse pad bought for $5 does not cost you ₹425 once freight,
  duty and the clearing agent are paid. It may cost ₹540.
- Buying from abroad in USD, CNY or another currency, at the rate on your Bill of Entry.
- Sharing one container's freight and customs across several orders and suppliers.
- Adding a bill that arrives weeks later, without redoing anything.

## Suppliers

Suppliers have their own page: [Suppliers](/merchant-guide/stock/suppliers).

## Adding products

In the **Products** card, click **Add products**. The same picker you use for collections
opens: search by name or SKU, filter by collection, type or status, and tick whole products or
just some of their variants. Click **Add** and each ticked variant becomes a line with quantity
1. When the order is in rupees, the unit price starts at the product's current cost, so you
only change what is different. A variant that is already on the order is not added twice.

## Currency and exchange rate

In the **Currency** card, pick what the supplier bills in. For anything other than rupees,
enter the **exchange rate**: how many rupees one unit of that currency is. Use the rate on your
Bill of Entry (customs uses it) or on your bank's payment advice. Unit prices on the order are
then typed in that currency, and every total is shown in rupees.

The currency is fixed once the order is sent. The rate can still be corrected until the order
is finished.

## Charges

Everything you pay on top of the goods is a **charge**. Add them in the **Charges** card on a
purchase order, or as **shared charges** on an inbound shipment.

| Charge | Part of product cost? |
| --- | --- |
| Freight and insurance | Yes |
| Basic customs duty (BCD) | Yes |
| Social welfare surcharge (SWS) | Yes |
| IGST paid at customs | **No**: you claim it back as input credit |
| Clearing agent (CHA) fee | Yes (its GST is input credit) |
| Port and handling | Yes |
| Local freight | Yes |
| Bank and exchange charges | Yes |
| Goods adjustment | Yes. Can be negative, for a supplier credit |
| Other | Yes, unless you switch it off |

For each charge, type the amount **before GST** and, separately, the GST on that bill. GST on a
bill is always input credit, so it is tracked but never added to product cost. IGST paid at
customs is marked **Claim back as input credit** for the same reason. You can switch that on or
off for any charge.

## How a charge is split across products

| Split | Use it for |
| --- | --- |
| **By weight** | Freight and insurance (the default). Heavier products carry more. |
| **By value** | Customs duty and most other charges (the default). Dearer products carry more. |
| **By quantity** | An equal share per unit. |
| **Only some products** | A charge that belongs to a few products only, split by value among them. |
| **Amount per product** | When the bill already says the amount per product, like duty per line on a Bill of Entry. The amounts must add up to the charge. |

If a product has **no weight** saved, a by-weight charge is split **by value** instead, and the
Charges card says which products need a weight. Type each product's weight per unit (in grams,
from the supplier's packing list) in the **Weight (g)** column of the order's Products card, or
on the product page. It saves onto the product as soon as you leave the field, works even after
the goods have arrived, and the split follows.

Splits are worked out in whole paise and always add up to the charge exactly.

## Inbound shipments

One container can carry several purchase orders. See [Inbound shipments](/merchant-guide/stock/inbound-shipments).

An order on a shipment can still change (add or remove products, change quantities or prices)
until goods on it start arriving. When you save, the shipment's shared charges split again
across every order on it. Once something has arrived, its products, quantities and prices are
fixed.

## Upload bills and let Koomerce read them

Instead of typing every line, upload the bill and let Koomerce read it for you. You always check
what it read before anything changes.

1. Open the purchase order (or the inbound shipment) and scroll to **Bills and documents**.
2. Pick **What is it**: Supplier invoice, Bill of Entry, Freight bill, Clearing agent (CHA) bill,
   Bank or exchange advice, or Other bill.
3. Drop the file in, or click to choose it. PDF, JPG or PNG, up to 15 MB. A phone photo of a
   paper bill works too.
4. It shows **Reading…** for a few seconds, then **Ready to check**. Click **Check and apply**.

The check screen shows the file on the left and what Koomerce read on the right. Anything it was
not sure about says **Check this**. Correct any value, untick what you do not want, then press
**Apply**. Nothing is saved until you do, and everything you ticked is saved together.

### What each bill does

| Bill | What it can apply |
| --- | --- |
| **Supplier invoice** | On a draft order: the quantities and prices per product (new products found by their SKU are added), and the currency and exchange rate. On an order you already sent: a **goods adjustment** for any price difference, because the order itself is locked. Freight or insurance printed on the invoice becomes a charge. |
| **Bill of Entry** | The exchange rate customs used, onto every order in the shipment priced in that currency; the BE number, date and port, onto the shipment; and BCD, SWS and IGST as charges. When every line is matched, each product gets exactly the duty printed for it. IGST stays out of cost (input credit). |
| **Freight, CHA, bank or other bill** | One charge with its amount before GST and the GST (input credit). You can change which kind of charge it is. |

### How products are matched

For each line on the bill, Koomerce looks for the product on your order by the supplier's code or
SKU first, then by HSN code and name. If it cannot tell, the line says **Not matched**: pick the
product yourself, or leave it unticked. Koomerce never creates new products from a bill.

Differences are shown on each line, for example *Order says 100 @ $12.50, this says 98 @ $12.40*.
On a sent order, quantity differences are settled when you receive: receive what arrived and
close the order.

### Good to know

- Each bill applies **once**. To apply it again (say you removed what it added by hand), open it
  and click **Allow applying again**. That does not undo anything.
- The file stays private to your store. It is never on a public link.
- Reading a bill counts as one AI request in your monthly allowance (Settings → AI). Turn
  **Read bills with AI** off there to keep files without reading them.
- If a bill could not be read, click **Read again**, or upload a clearer photo or the original
  PDF.

## Print or email the purchase order

Once the order is saved, the **⋯** menu at the top has:

- **Print**: opens the purchase order as a PDF in a new tab, ready to print.
- **Download PDF**: saves the same file.
- **Email to supplier**: sends the PDF to the supplier from your store's email address.

The PDF shows your business (with the GSTIN of the warehouse it is going to), the address to
deliver to, the supplier, each product with its SKU and HSN code, quantity and price in the
order's currency, the total, the date you want it by, the supplier's payment terms and your
notes. Freight, duty and other charges are **not** on it: those are your own costs.

To email it, check the supplier's address (it is filled in from the order), add a message if
you like, for example *Please pack the sleeves separately*, and click **Send**. On a draft,
**Also mark as ordered** is ticked, so the units start showing as incoming at the same moment.
If the email cannot be sent, nothing changes and the draft stays a draft. Replies from the
supplier come straight to your inbox.

The email goes from the sender address you set up under **Settings › Notifications**. Until
that address is verified, it goes from the platform's address with replies set to your store's
email.

## Receiving

Click **Receive items**. For each product, enter how many you **accept** (they go on hand) and
how many you **reject** (damaged or wrong; they stop showing as incoming but are not stocked).
The dialog shows what the product's cost becomes before you confirm.

### How the product cost changes

The new cost is the **weighted average** of what you already had and what just arrived:

> new cost = (units on hand × current cost + units accepted × landed cost) ÷ (units on hand + units accepted)

If you had none on hand, the landed cost simply becomes the cost.

**Example.** You have 10 pads at ₹500. You receive 10 more that landed at ₹800 each.
New cost = (10 × 500 + 10 × 800) ÷ 20 = **₹650**.

## A bill that arrives later

Clearing agents and freight forwarders often bill after the goods are in. Just add the charge to
the order or shipment, even if it is already received. The landed cost is worked out again, and
the extra cost is added to the units **still on your shelf**. Units you already sold keep the
cost they were sold at.

**Example.** You received 10 pads at ₹1,000 and sold 4. A ₹1,000 CHA bill arrives: that is ₹100
a pad. The 6 still on hand go up to ₹1,100. Fix or remove the charge later and the cost moves
back the same way.

## Cancel, close and reopen

- **Cancel order**: for an order nothing has arrived on yet. It stops showing as incoming and
  stops sharing any shipment's charges.
- **Close**: stop waiting for the rest of a partly received order.
- **Reopen**: undo a close (if something is still outstanding) or a cancel.
- **Duplicate** (in the **⋯** menu): a new draft with the same supplier, destination, currency,
  exchange rate and products, for a restock that repeats. Change what you need and send it.

## Good to know

- Old purchase orders: their Shipping and Taxes fields became charges. Shipping counts as cost;
  Taxes were GST, so they are marked as input credit.
- Stock received before landed cost existed keeps its old cost. Only receipts from now on are
  averaged in.
