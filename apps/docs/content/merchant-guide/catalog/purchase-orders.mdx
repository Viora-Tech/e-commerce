# Purchase orders and landed cost

A **purchase order** records what you ordered from a supplier. When the stock arrives, you
receive it, and two things happen: the units go on your shelf, and each product's **cost** is
updated to what it really cost you to get it there: the price you paid **plus** its share of
freight, customs duty and clearing. That true cost is the **landed cost**.

You find all of this under **Products**: **Purchase orders**, **Inbound shipments** and
**Suppliers**.

## What this is for

- Knowing your real margin. A mouse pad bought for $5 does not cost you ₹425 once freight,
  duty and the clearing agent are paid. It may cost ₹540.
- Buying from abroad in USD, CNY or another currency, at the rate on your Bill of Entry.
- Sharing one container's freight and customs across several orders and suppliers.
- Adding a bill that arrives weeks later, without redoing anything.

## Suppliers

Save each supplier once under **Products › Suppliers**: name, country, the currency they bill
in, GSTIN (for Indian suppliers), contact details and payment terms. On a purchase order,
search for the supplier or click **New supplier**. A new order starts in that supplier's
currency.

## Currency and exchange rate

In the **Currency** card, pick what the supplier bills in. For anything other than rupees,
enter the **exchange rate**: how many rupees one unit of that currency is. Use the rate on your
Bill of Entry (customs uses it) or on your bank's payment advice. Unit prices on the order are
then typed in that currency, and every total is shown in rupees.

The currency is fixed once the order is sent. The rate can still be corrected until the order
is finished.

## Charges

Everything you pay on top of the goods is a **charge**. Add them in the **Charges** card on a
purchase order, or as **shared charges** on an inbound shipment.

| Charge | Part of product cost? |
| --- | --- |
| Freight and insurance | Yes |
| Basic customs duty (BCD) | Yes |
| Social welfare surcharge (SWS) | Yes |
| IGST paid at customs | **No**: you claim it back as input credit |
| Clearing agent (CHA) fee | Yes (its GST is input credit) |
| Port and handling | Yes |
| Local freight | Yes |
| Bank and exchange charges | Yes |
| Goods adjustment | Yes. Can be negative, for a supplier credit |
| Other | Yes, unless you switch it off |

For each charge, type the amount **before GST** and, separately, the GST on that bill. GST on a
bill is always input credit, so it is tracked but never added to product cost. IGST paid at
customs is marked **Claim back as input credit** for the same reason. You can switch that on or
off for any charge.

## How a charge is split across products

| Split | Use it for |
| --- | --- |
| **By weight** | Freight and insurance (the default). Heavier products carry more. |
| **By value** | Customs duty and most other charges (the default). Dearer products carry more. |
| **By quantity** | An equal share per unit. |
| **Only some products** | A charge that belongs to a few products only, split by value among them. |
| **Amount per product** | When the bill already says the amount per product, like duty per line on a Bill of Entry. The amounts must add up to the charge. |

If a product has **no weight** saved, a by-weight charge is split **by value** instead, and the
Charges card says which products need a weight. Add weights to the products (on the product
page) and the split follows.

Splits are worked out in whole paise and always add up to the charge exactly.

## Inbound shipments

One container, air shipment or Bill of Entry often carries **several purchase orders**, even
from different suppliers. Create it under **Products › Inbound shipments**, add the purchase
orders that came in it, and put the container's freight and customs on the shipment as
**shared charges**. They are split across every product in every order on it.

## Receiving

Click **Receive items**. For each product, enter how many you **accept** (they go on hand) and
how many you **reject** (damaged or wrong; they stop showing as incoming but are not stocked).
The dialog shows what the product's cost becomes before you confirm.

### How the product cost changes

The new cost is the **weighted average** of what you already had and what just arrived:

> new cost = (units on hand × current cost + units accepted × landed cost) ÷ (units on hand + units accepted)

If you had none on hand, the landed cost simply becomes the cost.

**Example.** You have 10 pads at ₹500. You receive 10 more that landed at ₹800 each.
New cost = (10 × 500 + 10 × 800) ÷ 20 = **₹650**.

## A bill that arrives later

Clearing agents and freight forwarders often bill after the goods are in. Just add the charge to
the order or shipment, even if it is already received. The landed cost is worked out again, and
the extra cost is added to the units **still on your shelf**. Units you already sold keep the
cost they were sold at.

**Example.** You received 10 pads at ₹1,000 and sold 4. A ₹1,000 CHA bill arrives: that is ₹100
a pad. The 6 still on hand go up to ₹1,100. Fix or remove the charge later and the cost moves
back the same way.

## Cancel, close and reopen

- **Cancel order**: for an order nothing has arrived on yet. It stops showing as incoming and
  stops sharing any shipment's charges.
- **Close**: stop waiting for the rest of a partly received order.
- **Reopen**: undo a close (if something is still outstanding) or a cancel.

## Good to know

- Old purchase orders: their Shipping and Taxes fields became charges. Shipping counts as cost;
  Taxes were GST, so they are marked as input credit.
- Stock received before landed cost existed keeps its old cost. Only receipts from now on are
  averaged in.
