# Product photos and videos

## What this is for

- Understanding why a photo looks slightly different (smaller, sharper edges) a moment after
  you upload it.
- Knowing the size limits before an upload is rejected.
- Knowing that your phone's exact location is removed from every photo automatically.

## What happens to a photo after you upload it

Every photo you add — to a product, a collection, a blog post — is cleaned up automatically
before it is shown anywhere:

- **Your phone's location is removed.** Phones save GPS coordinates inside the photo file
  (this is what lets your Photos app show a map of where a picture was taken). That location
  data never appears on your store, and never stays reachable after processing finishes — it
  is removed, not just hidden.
- **It's turned the right way up.** A sideways photo is straightened automatically.
- **It's resized for the web.** A giant 12-megapixel phone photo is shrunk to the largest size
  a screen actually needs, and several smaller copies are made for phones, tablets and
  thumbnails — so a shopper on a slow connection downloads a small file, not your original.
- **It's compressed further, invisibly.** Modern, smaller formats are generated alongside the
  original; browsers that support them get a noticeably smaller download with no visible
  quality loss.

This takes a few seconds after you upload. While it's happening, the photo shows a
"Processing" state in the media library; once it's done, it shows normally everywhere.
If something goes wrong (a corrupted file, for example), it shows "Failed" and you can
delete and re-upload it.

Animated GIFs are converted to a modern animated format automatically, which is usually much
smaller for the same look; if that ever causes a problem with a specific GIF, contact support.

## Size limits

| Type | Limit |
| --- | --- |
| Photos (JPEG, PNG, WebP, AVIF, GIF) | 10 MB |
| Video (MP4 only) | 100 MB |

SVG files are never accepted, on any plan — they can carry code, not just a picture, so
allowing them would be a security risk to your store and your shoppers.

## Privacy

Because location data is removed from every photo, it is safe to upload photos taken on a
phone directly — you do not need to strip location data yourself first, and you do not need
to worry about a customer being able to find out where a product photo was taken.
