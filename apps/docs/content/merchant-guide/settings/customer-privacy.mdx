# Privacy & data requests

**Settings → Privacy & data requests** is where your store meets India's data protection law, the
Digital Personal Data Protection Act 2023 (DPDP Act). It has three parts: the cookie banner,
data requests from customers, and your grievance officer.

<Callout type="warning">
This guide explains what the store does for you. It is not legal advice. If you are unsure
about your own situation, ask a lawyer.
</Callout>

## Your DPDP checklist

Work down this list once. Most of it takes a few minutes.

<Steps>

### Publish a privacy policy

In **Settings → Policies**, create the **Privacy policy** from its template, read it, and
publish it. It says what you collect, why, who you share it with, how long you keep it and
how customers use their rights.

### Name a grievance officer

Also in **Settings → Policies**. This is the person customers contact about their data. It
can be you. Their name and contact details show in your privacy policy and store footer.

### Leave the cookie banner on

It is always on. Shoppers choose **Accept all**, **Necessary only** or pick for themselves.
Until they say yes, your store analytics, Google Analytics, Tag Manager and the Meta pixel
do not load. If you add a new tag or change what the banner says, press
**Ask everyone again**.

### Only message people who said yes

Campaigns and automations reach a customer only on the channels they agreed to: email,
WhatsApp or SMS. Customers say yes at signup, in their account, or tell you directly (then you
switch it on in their customer page). Anyone who unsubscribes gets nothing more. You do not
need to do anything to enforce this: the store checks every message before it goes.

The email signup box on your store asks people to confirm first. They get one short email with
a **Yes, subscribe me** button; only after they press it do they join your list and get the
welcome message. Someone who types another person's address cannot sign them up.

### Answer data requests on time

Customers can ask for a copy of their data or to delete it. You get each one in
**Customer privacy → Data requests** with a due date. Answer before it.

### Choose how customers sign in

In **Settings → Customer accounts**, pick email, SMS or WhatsApp codes and whether guests can
check out. Customers can delete their own account from their account page.

</Steps>

## The cookie banner

Your store needs a few cookies to work, like the cart and sign in. These are always on.
Everything else is optional and off until the shopper says yes:

| Category | What it covers | Loads after |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Necessary | Cart, checkout, sign in, security | Always on |
| Analytics | Your store's visit counts and traffic sources, Google Analytics, Tag Manager | "Analytics" or "Accept all" |
| Marketing | Meta pixel and other ad measurement | "Marketing" or "Accept all" |

- **Banner message**: leave empty for the standard wording, or write your own.
- **Ask everyone again**: every shopper sees the banner again on their next visit, and
  optional tags wait for a new answer. Use it after adding a tag or changing the wording.
- The page shows how shoppers answered in the last 30 days.
- Shoppers change their mind with **Cookie settings** at the bottom of every page.

The store keeps a record of every answer, with the time and the banner version, as proof of
consent.

<Callout>
Your store's own visit counts (Analytics → Live view, sales by source) only count shoppers who
allowed analytics. Numbers can look lower than before the banner. That is expected.
</Callout>

## Data requests

A customer can ask for:

- **A copy of their data**: their profile, addresses, orders and consent history.
- **Deletion**: their personal data erased.

Requests come from the **Your data** page in their account, or you log them when a customer
writes or calls: **Data requests → Log a request**.

Each request gets a due date from **Days to answer a request** (30 days by default, between 7
and 90). The queue shows the soonest first, and marks requests that are due soon or late.

### Answer a request for a copy

1. Open the request and press **Mark in progress**.
2. Press **Download JSON** or **Download spreadsheet (CSV)**.
3. Check the file and send it to the customer from your own email.
4. Press **Mark done**.

### Answer a deletion request

1. Open the request. If the customer is not matched, find them first or decline.
2. Press **Erase customer data** and confirm.

This cannot be undone. The store erases their name, email, phone, saved addresses, notes and
marketing preferences, and signs them out everywhere. Orders and GST invoices keep the buyer
name, address, GSTIN and amounts, because tax law requires those records for years. Orders
older than eight years lose those too.

You can also **Decline** a request, with a reason you could send the customer.

### Everything is recorded

Every step (who logged a request, who downloaded a copy, who erased data, and when) is kept in
the request's **History** and cannot be changed afterwards.

## Marketing consent on a customer

Each customer page has a **Marketing consent** card with a switch for email, WhatsApp and
SMS. Only switch one on when the customer told you yes. Every change is recorded with who made
it and when. The same card lets you download the customer's data or log a request for them.
