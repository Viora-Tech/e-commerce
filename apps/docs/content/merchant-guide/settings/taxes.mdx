# Taxes & GST

**Settings → Taxes & GST → GST & invoices** is one page with three tabs:

| Tab | What it holds | Used to be |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **GST numbers** | Your business and PAN, a GST number for each state you sell from, and which one is the default. | **Settings → Business and GST** |
| **Tax rules** | Your registered legal name and address, whether prices include GST, the default rate and HSN code, the composition scheme. | **Settings → Taxes & GST** |
| **Invoice numbers** | How your tax invoices are numbered, and e-invoicing. | **Settings → Taxes & GST** |

Old links to either page still work and open the right tab. The tab you are on is part of the
address (`?tab=tax-rules`), so you can bookmark it or send it to your CA.

## What this is for

- Keeping every GST number your business holds in one place, and choosing the **default** one.
- Putting your GST number, legal name and registered address on your invoices.
- Deciding how much GST each order carries when a product has no rate of its own.
- Numbering your tax invoices the way GST expects: every number used once, in order.

If your business is not registered for GST, you can skip this page. Nothing on it is needed.

## How GST works here

The **Tax rules** tab ends with a short guide. The same points, in brief:

- **Inside your state: CGST + SGST.** When the delivery address is in the same state as your
  GST registration, the tax is split in half: CGST to the central government, SGST to your
  state.
- **Outside your state: IGST.** When it is another state, it is one tax, IGST, at the full rate.
  The delivery address decides which one; you do not pick it.
- **Prices including or excluding GST.** Including means the price already has the tax inside
  it and the customer pays exactly that. Excluding means tax is added on top.

A ₹1,000 item at 18%:

| | Price includes GST | Price excludes GST |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Item value | ₹847.46 | ₹1,000 |
| GST | ₹152.54 | ₹180 |
| Customer pays | ₹1,000 | ₹1,180 |
| Same state | CGST ₹76.27 + SGST ₹76.27 | CGST ₹90 + SGST ₹90 |
| Other state | IGST ₹152.54 | IGST ₹180 |

The example on the Tax rules tab uses your own default rate.

## GST per state

Each order is billed from the GST number of the warehouse its products sell from (you choose
it per location on [Locations](/merchant-guide/settings/locations)). If that warehouse has no
GST number chosen, the order is billed from your default GST number.

Your prices already include GST, so this never changes what the shopper pays. It only changes
how the tax is split on the invoice:

| Warehouse in | Shopper in | Tax on the order |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Haryana | Haryana | CGST + SGST |
| Haryana | Karnataka | IGST |
| Karnataka | Karnataka | CGST + SGST |

The order page shows which GST number it was billed from, for example **Billed from:
Haryana · 06AAPFU0939F1ZV**. Each GST number also numbers its own invoices and files its own
GSTR-1 (see [GST invoices](/merchant-guide/money/gst-invoices#more-than-one-gst-number)).

## GST numbers tab

In India you get one GST number for each state where you have a shop or warehouse. If you send
orders from Haryana and from Delhi, you have two GST numbers, and both belong to the same PAN.

- **Your business** shows your legal name and PAN. You do not type the PAN for your first GST
  number: we read it from the GST number itself (characters 3 to 12 of a GST number are the PAN).
- **GST numbers** lists each number with its state. We read the state from the first two
  digits, so you never pick it. The default one has a **Default** badge.
- **Add GST number** asks for the 15-character number from your GST certificate. As you type,
  the page shows "Registered in Haryana" (or your state). The other fields are optional: an
  invoice prefix such as `HR` (printed where your numbering uses `{GST}`), and the address on
  the certificate, which invoices from this GST number print as your address.
- If the number belongs to a different PAN, you see: *"This GST number belongs to a different
  PAN. Is it another business?"* Check for a typo first. If it really is another business, use
  **Add it as another business**.
- **Make default** (from the ⋮ menu) switches your default GST number. **Mark as cancelled** is
  only for a number the GST department has cancelled; old invoices keep showing it.

### More than one business

Open **Advanced: more than one business** only if a warehouse belongs to a different legal
business (a different PAN), for example a partner's stock you sell.

> Selling stock owned by another business makes you an e-commerce operator. Talk to your CA
> first.

### GST numbers and the Tax rules tab

Once you have a GST number on the GST numbers tab, the **Tax rules** tab shows it read-only with
a **Manage GST numbers** button that brings you back to the first tab. If you have no GST
number yet, typing one on Tax rules adds it as your first.

Only owners and administrators can change this tab. Staff cannot open it.

## Tax rules tab

<Steps>

### GST registration

Type your 15-character **GSTIN**, like `06AAACW1234A1Z5`. The page checks it as you save: a
typo in any character is caught by the check digit at the end. Once the first two digits are
in, it shows the state you are registered in, for example "Registered in Haryana".

Add your **Registered legal name** (the name on your GST certificate, which can differ from your
shop name) and your **Registered address**. Both are printed on invoices.

### How you charge GST

- **I am on the composition scheme**: turn this on only if you chose the composition scheme
  when you registered. No GST is added to your orders, and your invoices become a "Bill of
  Supply" with the words "Composition taxable person, not eligible to collect tax on supplies".
- **My prices include GST**: on by default, the usual way in India. The price you type is what
  the customer pays and GST is worked out from inside it.

  <Callout type="warning">
  Adding GST on top of your prices at checkout is not built yet. You can switch this off to
  record how you price, but for now customers still pay the price you type.
  </Callout>

- **Default GST rate**: used for any product that has no rate of its own. Since 22 September
  2025 most goods are 5% or 18%.
- **Default HSN code**: the goods code printed on invoices for products without their own.
  4 digits up to ₹5 crore turnover, 6 above.

A different rate or HSN code for one product is set on that product's page in **Products**.

### Save

Select **Save** in the bar at the top. You will see "Tax settings saved". The bar stays put
when you switch tabs, so you can change a rule and an invoice number and save both at once.

</Steps>

## Invoice numbers tab

<Steps>

### Invoice numbers

- **Starts with**: letters, numbers, `/` and `-`. Write `{FY}` where you want the year:
  `INV/{FY}/` gives `INV/26-27/0001`.
- **Next number**: moving from another system? Start where your old invoices stopped. You can
  only go up, never back to a number that is already used.
- **Start again at 1 every April**: on by default. A new financial year starts on 1 April; most
  shops restart the count then and keep the year in the number.

The line under the fields shows your next invoice number. These are the same numbers the
**GST invoices** app uses; if the app is not installed yet, the page says so and links to
**Apps**. Separate numbers for exports, credit notes or business buyers are set in
**Apps → GST invoices → Settings**.

### E-invoicing (IRN)

Not built yet. Businesses above ₹5 crore turnover must get an IRN and QR code from the
government portal for B2B invoices; for now, do that on the e-invoice portal yourself.

### Save

Select **Save** in the bar at the top. You will see "Tax settings saved".

</Steps>

## Try it

1. Open **Settings → GST & invoices**. It opens on **GST numbers**.
2. Select **Add GST number**, type your GST number and check the state it shows.
3. Add a second one for another state, then use **⋮ → Make default** to switch.
4. Open the **Tax rules** tab and check **My prices include GST** and the default rate.
5. Open the **Invoice numbers** tab and check the next invoice number under the fields.
6. Open **Settings → Locations & warehouses** and pick which GST number each location bills under.

## Common questions

**I changed the legal name here and it changed in the GST invoices app too. Is that right?**
Yes. Both screens edit the same details, so your invoices always match.

**Does changing the default rate change old orders?** No. Each order keeps the tax it was
placed with. The new rate applies to orders placed after you save.
