# Live view globe

The admin draws the last 24 hours of orders on a dotted globe in two places:

| Where | Renderer | Why |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Home → Live view card | [cobe](https://github.com/shuding/cobe) (MIT), patched with a detailed land map | Small card, already cached on Home; cobe's ~32k-dot ceiling is fine at that size. |
| Analytics → Live view (`/analytics/live`) | Our own WebGL globe, `live-globe/webgl-globe-canvas.tsx` | Full-page hero where merchants zoom in on India; cobe's dots overlap and smear past ~32k. |

Both take the same props (`GlobeCanvasProps` in `live-globe/types.ts`) and share the motion
and input code (`globe-controls.ts`: ease onto India, auto-rotate, drag to spin and tilt with
inertia, wheel / trackpad / touch-pinch zoom, reduced motion), the palette
(`globe-palette.ts`, read from the `--muted-foreground`, `--primary` and `--chart-2` tokens)
and the overlay (`globe-overlay.tsx`: one focusable button per marker, the tooltip, the
zoom buttons). Only the drawing differs.

## Our WebGL globe

No three.js: about 6 KB minified for the engine (`webgl/`), 12 KB for the whole lazy chunk
including the shared controls and overlay.

- **Land dots on the CPU** (`webgl/land-dots.ts`). cobe finds each pixel's nearest
  Fibonacci-sphere point inside its fragment shader in 32-bit floats; we place each point once
  in JavaScript (doubles), test it against the 2048×1024 land map and upload the land points as
  a static vertex buffer. The Fibonacci walk moves down the map one row at a time, so no `asin`
  is needed per point: the finest level (1.08M sphere points, ~320k land dots) builds in about
  20 ms.
- **Levels of detail.** `LOD_SAMPLES` is 40k / 120k / 360k / 1.08M sphere points.
  `pickLevel(radius)` picks the coarsest level whose dots are at most ~3.4 CSS px apart, so
  zooming in swaps to more, smaller dots. The top level spaces dots about one map pixel
  (0.18°, 20 km) apart; India goes from ~390 dots with cobe to ~13k. Levels are built on first
  use and cached for the page session; the next finer level is prebuilt 400 ms after a switch.
  Zoom goes to 4× (cobe: 2.6×); markers and arcs keep their zoom-1 size past 1×.
- **Renderer** (`webgl/globe-renderer.ts`, WebGL 1). One full-screen triangle shades the
  sphere and the halo, then point sprites draw the land dots (with the sphere's shading, so
  they fade into it at the rim), the arcs (cobe's quadratic curve, drawn as a chain of dots)
  and the markers. Orthographic projection; anything behind the disc is dropped in the vertex
  shader. `rotation()` / `anglesFor()` are the same maths the overlay uses to place the marker
  buttons, so tooltips sit exactly on their markers.
- **Lazy loading.** `live-globe-hero.tsx` imports `webgl-globe-canvas` with a dynamic
  `import()` two animation frames after mount, so the stats cards paint first and nothing of
  the globe is in the Home bundle. The land map (`apps/admin/public/globe/land-2048.png`,
  20 KB) is fetched when the globe starts; until it arrives the sphere, markers and arcs draw
  without land.

## The land map

Natural Earth 1:50m land (public domain; "Made with Natural Earth"), rasterised by
`scripts/globe/make-land-map.py` to a 1-bit 2048×1024 equirectangular PNG. The same image
is inlined in `patches/cobe@2.0.1.patch` for the Home card. To rebuild it, run the script
(see its docstring), copy the PNG to `apps/admin/public/globe/land-2048.png`, and regenerate
the cobe patch.

## Tests

`webgl/land-dots.test.ts` covers the land lookup, dot placement past 32k points, level
picking, the rotation (the focus point faces the viewer; east is right, north is up) and the
arc and marker point buffers. WebGL itself is not exercised in unit tests.
