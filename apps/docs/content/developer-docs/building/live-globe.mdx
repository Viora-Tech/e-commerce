# Live view globe

The admin draws the last 24 hours of orders on a globe in two places:

| Where | Renderer | Why |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Home → Live view card | [cobe](https://github.com/shuding/cobe) (MIT), patched with a detailed land map | Small card, already cached on Home; cobe's ~32k-dot ceiling is fine at that size. |
| Analytics → Live view (`/analytics/live`) | Our own WebGL globe, `live-globe/webgl-globe-canvas.tsx` | Full-page hero where merchants zoom in on India. Solid land instead of dots: any dot pattern shimmered (moiré) at full-page size. |

Both take the same props (`GlobeCanvasProps` in `live-globe/types.ts`) and share the motion
and input code (`globe-controls.ts`: ease onto India, auto-rotate, drag to spin and tilt with
inertia, wheel / trackpad / touch-pinch zoom, reduced motion), the palette
(`globe-palette.ts`, read from the `--muted-foreground`, `--primary` and `--chart-2` tokens)
and the overlay (`globe-overlay.tsx`: one focusable button per marker, the tooltip, the
zoom buttons). Only the drawing differs.

## Our WebGL globe

No three.js: about 6 KB minified for the engine (`webgl/`), 12 KB for the whole lazy chunk
including the shared controls and overlay.

- **Solid land, coast and borders in one shader** (`webgl/globe-renderer.ts`, WebGL 1 +
  `OES_standard_derivatives`). One full-screen triangle shades the sphere and the halo. Each
  pixel is turned back into latitude/longitude and reads the map texture (mipmapped, so it
  stays clean at every size): red is land, drawn darker than the ocean; the coast is where red
  crosses 0.5, and dividing by `fwidth` turns that into an on-screen distance, so the coastline
  is about one pixel wide at any zoom. Green holds country borders blurred on purpose: the
  shader turns the blurred value back into a distance (`sqrt(-2 ln g)`) in screen pixels for
  the same constant width. Faint 15° grid lines use the same trick. The longitude seam (where
  `atan` jumps from π to −π) would make the mipmap blur a line down the 180° meridian, so two
  u coordinates with seams half a turn apart are computed and the continuous one is used.
  Coast, borders and grid fade towards the rim. Zoom goes to 4× (cobe: 2.6×).
- **Markers and arcs** are point sprites on top (arcs are cobe's quadratic curve drawn as a
  chain of dots). Orthographic projection; anything behind the disc is dropped in the vertex
  shader. `rotation()` / `anglesFor()` are the same maths the overlay uses to place the marker
  buttons, so tooltips sit exactly on their markers.
- **Lazy loading.** `live-globe-hero.tsx` imports `webgl-globe-canvas` with a dynamic
  `import()` two animation frames after mount, so the stats cards paint first and nothing of
  the globe is in the Home bundle. The map (`apps/admin/public/globe/globe-2048.png`, about
  330 KB) is fetched when the globe starts; until it arrives the sphere, markers and arcs draw
  without land.

## The maps

**Live view:** Natural Earth 1:10m admin-0 countries, **India point of view** (public domain;
"Made with Natural Earth"), rasterised by `scripts/globe/make-globe-map.py` to a 2048×1024
equirectangular RGB PNG: red = land, green = borders between countries (outlines near the sea
are masked out; coasts come from the red channel). The point-of-view dataset draws India's
boundary as the Government of India does — all of Jammu & Kashmir and Ladakh (including
Aksai Chin) and Arunachal Pradesh. Never replace it with the default-view file. No border is drawn around India at all (the owner's
choice): the script masks out a band along India's outline, so its land borders with China,
Pakistan, Nepal, Bhutan, Bangladesh and Myanmar never appear. To rebuild,
see the script's docstring.

**Home card:** Natural Earth 1:50m land, rasterised by `scripts/globe/make-land-map.py` to a
1-bit 2048×1024 PNG inlined in `patches/cobe@2.0.1.patch` (cobe draws land only, no borders).

## Tests

`webgl/globe-renderer.test.ts` covers the rotation (the focus point faces the viewer; east is
right, north is up) and the arc and marker point buffers. The shader itself is not exercised
in unit tests; check `/analytics/live` after changing it.
